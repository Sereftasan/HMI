Profiles

The Profiles page keeps every profile the machine processes, with its drawing, the points its hardware is measured from, the accessories it takes and the programs made from them. It is a library: nothing on this page is sent to the PLC - it makes data and program files. Changing the library needs the Maintenance level; below it the page shows everything and changes nothing.

The panes. The table of profiles, the drawing, the details, the accessories and the programs are separate panes: drag them by the caption, resize from any edge, minimise with –. Save an arrangement by name and pick it from the Layout box; Default restores the built-in one, and your last arrangement returns by itself. 📚 opens the library's own tables.

How the library is filed. Vendor › series › variant › group › profile. The group says what a profile is (frame, sash, door leaf ...): accessories are given to a group once, and every profile of the group takes them. The series gives the material (PVC, aluminium), which chooses the feeds. A profile can be based on another (📑 in the table): it takes the other's group, sizes and reference points, and only what differs is entered.

The drawing. Each profile has its section as a DXF file (Details › Profile › File). It is read in profile coordinates: Y across, 0 at the outside face on the left, and Z up, 0 at the bed. A drawing made the other way round is turned or mirrored (Details, or ⟳ ⟲ ⇋ on the drawing) - the page says when the drawing's size disagrees with the profile's and offers Turn it. Layers that are not the profile (dimensions, a gasket drawn beside it) are listed under Not the profile: they are drawn grey and never cut through. Press 💾 to keep the details.

Reference points (Details › Reference points): the outside and inside face, the centre, the bed, the hardware axis, the glazing reference ... 🔍 finds them on the drawing, in this order of trust:

  • a layer named after the point (REF_HARDWARE_AXIS) - its point, circle or line;
  • a text naming it: HARDWARE_AXIS 22 35 gives the coordinates, several in one text count (OUTSIDE_SURFACE 0 0 INSIDE_SURFACE 70 0);
  • the outline: the faces, the centre and the bed;
  • the hardware groove seen from above, as wide as the group's hardware interface;
  • a similar profile's point, at the same distance from the nearer face (a proposal).

A point can also be typed (double-click Y or Z) or picked on the drawing (🎯, then click; it snaps to the nearest line end). ✔ confirms the selected points. Points confirmed or set by hand are never replaced by a new search.

Nothing unconfirmed is cut in production. A placement measured from a point nobody has confirmed stays proposed; one measured from confirmed points is worked out, fine for a trial piece. Only a placement a person confirmed goes into a production program.

Accessories. The upper table lists the accessories of the profile's group (➕ gives one to the group - those that fit its hardware interface come first; the tick switches one off without removing it). The lower table lists where each operation lands on this profile: the cut, the face the tool comes in by, Y and Z, the correction, where along the bar, and the depth with the walls the tool goes through. The selected one is drawn on the section: the arrow the tool comes in by, the cut in red and the walls in yellow.

Trial and tune. Move the selected placement with the Y and Z buttons (±0.1, 0.5, 1 mm) - the drawing shows where it would go. Keep the move for this profile, every profile of its group, or every profile of its series, for this operation or all its operations, and press 💾: the move is kept as a correction with its reason, and the accessory's own definition is not touched. A moved placement is to be confirmed again. ✖ marks an operation that is not on this profile, ↺ reopens a decision, 🕘 shows the history and 🧮 lists the corrections that reach the profile (and takes one away).

Programs.

  • Accessories: the side machining of one bar of the given length. ⚙ makes it with the confirmed placements - tick trial to include the unconfirmed ones for a test piece. Each operation takes the best mounted tool (a drill of the hole's size, else the largest end mill that fits), goes down in passes, enters each wall at its entry speed (slower still through a thin wall) and each chamber at the approach feed, and cuts at the wall's feed. What could not be made is listed in red under the program, with why. 💾 saves it as a file.
  • End milling: the contour milled around the profile's end, from the drawing. The drawing's tools choose and edit the border; the green band is the tool at its real width, and clicking it selects the matching line (and the other way round). 💾 keeps the program with the profile; 📤 saves it as a file.

The library's tables (📚): vendors, series, variants, groups, machining families and hardware interfaces; accessories with their operations (a macro, the face, the depth - the macro's, through the first wall or through the whole profile - the reference point, the offset and where along the bar), accessory groups, hardware vendors and series; macros, the shape of a cut only (no tool, no feed); and this machine's tools (their measured diameter and usable length - a tool with no diameter is never chosen), faces (which milling unit at which angle machines which face), feeds per material class, drawing layers that are another material (a steel reinforcement) and the program settings. Not confirmed yet lists every reference point and placement still to be checked; double-click one to open its profile.

The example catalogue offered when the library is new holds EXAMPLE positions, not a hardware maker's figures, and the machine faces are assumed (0° from above, 90° the outside face, 180° from below, 270° the inside face). Check both on the machine before the first program is run.

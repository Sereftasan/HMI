The old program's main control panel: Start (M21, set; lit while the machine works), Pause (M111, on and off) and Cancel (M24, set – asked first while the machine works, at once when it does not), and the speed of the work (D408), a slider through the old track bar's steps (0, 1, 2, 4 … 95, 100 %) with its percentage to type any value (5). The job list is not on the page.

Manual Movement

The tabs and their rows are those of the HMI program the machine ran before: Entry Conveyor, Robot, Profile Recognition, Milling, Cutting, Exit, Other, Clamping Moulds, YZ Cross Rollers. Each page reads top to bottom as the old table did, in columns; its sections are headings (Milling: Unit 1 and Unit 2 side by side; Cutting: Cutting and V Cutting). The old Back column is a unit's ◀ button, Forward its ▶, Positions the value beside them. A row with one button is a button; Change Degree and Drills to Changing Pos. are a group of small buttons.

The bar above the tabs, the same over every tab (and at the top of Machine Parameters › Axis and the Axis Monitor): the axes' manual speed (D409, a slider and its percentage), JOG – the axes move while their button is held (lit: M174) – or a step per press: 10, 1, 0.1 or 0.01 mm (M170 – M173; JOG takes them off). For Maintenance and Admin only: Safety Bypass (M175, on and off – switched off whenever the user changes) and, on Manual Movement, Cylinder FW/Back (4). The axes can be coupled only in step mode, JOG off (4).

Safety. Safety Bypass lets the PLC move what its interlocks would hold back. Use it only to free the machine, watching it, and switch it off at once.

The motors – the Entry Conveyor's, Milling 1 and 2, the Saw, the V Cutting saw, the Exit Conveyor's – are motor units (4c): their buttons, run lamp and value as one; an inverter's fault is not shown as a number but makes both buttons blink red, and its ? offers the fault's entry and the FR-A800 manual. The HMI raises their alarms itself (their alarm bits M827 – M831).

Clamping Moulds: a row for each mould, a pneumatic positioning (4a) – its manual movement (down / up for a vertical mould, back / forward for a horizontal one, and its position), then Target position and its start (the old Send To Position, for the operator), Calibration and Calibrate, Positioning tolerance and Direction ref (the old Stopping Offset: the PLC compares the target with it to choose the direction – a target above it is approached the way the position increases, one below it the other way) – Maintenance. Type a target, then press ▶| beside it: the mould goes there.

The six front vertical moulds are always fitted. The ten back moulds, vertical and horizontal, are fitted only with the Clamping Mould option (Machine Parameters › Others, Machine Options): without it they are not shown and the front ones stand together at the top of the page; tick the option and they appear in their places.

The back moulds' valves are wired to the second controller (PLC 2: Y0 – Y23), and so are the reference sensors of the three back horizontal moulds with an encoder – Profile Recognition, Cutting Left and Cutting Right Back Horizontal: X12, X13, X14 of PLC 2 (X12@2 on the page; PLC 1's encoder inputs were full). Their buttons are PLC 1's as the others'; their lamp, their help and the HMI's alarm for them read PLC 2, and say so (X12 (PLC 2)).

An axis unit (Robot X, Unit 1 X ...) takes its buttons, position and reference lamp from its row on Machine Parameters › Axis; change them there, not on the page. Robot X also has a linear encoder (D22, the old Encoders page's Robot Position): its Load Position, a choice of the Position heading on the Axis page and the Axis Monitor (10). The angle axes (Milling 1 and 2 Angle, Cutting Angle) have ◀ ▶; the vertical ones ▼ ▲, and only these are checked for a holding brake.

The clamps with an encoder (4b) – the Gripper, Profile Recognition's Front, Top and Left Top Clamping, Entry Conveyor's Profile Clamping 7, Milling's Top and Front Clamping, Cutting's Left Top, Right Front and Right Front Vert. Clamping, the Exit's Robot Top Clamping – take theirs from their rows on Machine Parameters › Encoders.

Machine Parameters

As on the old program, in these tabs: Axis (the axes' limits, reference, calibration and reference activation, in the old order: Robot, Milling 1, Milling 2, Cutting, Exit Robot, V Cutting, with the bar of the axes' speed and steps at the top; Send Axes to Reference under them sends the axes ticked in the Ref column to their references), Conveyor and Robot (the old Entry Parameters), Milling and Cutting (the old Process Parameters), Encoders, Others and Alarms. Every value is in the unit shown beside it (mm, s, °, %), divided as the old pages divided it. They need the Maintenance level.

Encoders: a row per encoder (10) – Robot Gripper, Profile Recognition Front and Top, Milling Top, Milling-Cutting Left Front, Cutting Left Top, Cutting Right Front, Exit Carrying Top, Cutting Front Right Vertical, Left Profile Recognition Front and Top – its manual movement (to move it while setting it up), Reference, Measuring tolerance, Minimum limit (the old Start Limit) and Unclamping interval. The old page's first row, Robot Position, is Robot X (an axis). The Robot Gripper's position shows two decimals, as the old Robot page did; its Reference (D516, the old Robot page's Encoder Reference) is set here only.

Others: the exit conveyor, the distances between the units, the lubrication, the Machine Options (a tick box per option the machine has: mullion notching, cooling, the carrying rollers, a reverse machine ...), the Middle Register Notch Parameters and the cross rollers' distances from the milling's X zero.

Safety. A machine option tells the PLC program what the machine has. Change one only when the machine was built or changed that way.

PLC labels and structured data types

The PLC Monitor (11) names this machine's devices from PlcLabels.csv and offers its structured data types from StructuredTypes.csv, both beside the program – for the tables of the Profiles & Operations sheet: Milling Steps (16 devices: the cooling and motor selection, X, Y, Z in 0.01 mm, i, j, k, the speed – unit 1's at R0, unit 2's at R12800, 400 each, and their spares), Profile Parameters (R25600, 50 devices), Milling Parameters (R26000, 100 of 10), Special Cut Parameters (R25650, 10 of 5), Special Cut Steps and Mullion Notching Step (both R25700, 30 of 10), and the job list's three columns (D5000, D6000, D7000, 500 double words each). A label of an array can be shown as a table, a line per step.

Messages from the PLC

The PLC asks for a message by writing its number to D71; the answer is written to D72 (Cancel / No 0, OK / Yes / Continue 1, Restart 2) and D71 set back to 0. The messages, in every language, are in Popups.json beside the program.

Profiles

The Profiles page keeps every profile the machine processes, with its drawing, the points its hardware is measured from, the accessories it takes and the programs made from them. It is a library: nothing on this page is sent to the PLC - it makes data and program files. Changing the library needs the Maintenance level; below it the page shows everything and changes nothing.

The panes. The table of profiles, the drawing, the details, the accessories and the programs are separate panes: drag them by the caption, resize from any edge, minimise with –. Save an arrangement by name and pick it from the Layout box; Default restores the built-in one, and your last arrangement returns by itself. 📚 opens the library's own tables.

How the library is filed. Vendor › series › variant › group › profile. The group says what a profile is (frame, sash, door leaf ...): accessories are given to a group once, and every profile of the group takes them. The series gives the material (PVC, aluminium), which chooses the feeds. A profile can be based on another (📑 in the table): it takes the other's group, sizes and reference points, and only what differs is entered.

The drawing. Each profile has its section as a DXF file (Details › Profile › File). It is read in profile coordinates: Y across, 0 at the outside face on the left, and Z up, 0 at the bed. A drawing made the other way round is turned or mirrored (Details, or ⟳ ⟲ ⇋ on the drawing) - the page says when the drawing's size disagrees with the profile's and offers Turn it. Layers that are not the profile (dimensions, a gasket drawn beside it) are listed under Not the profile: they are drawn grey and never cut through. Press 💾 to keep the details.

Reference points (Details › Reference points): the outside and inside face, the centre, the bed, the hardware axis, the glazing reference ... 🔍 finds them on the drawing, in this order of trust:

  • a layer named after the point (REF_HARDWARE_AXIS) - its point, circle or line;
  • a text naming it: HARDWARE_AXIS 22 35 gives the coordinates, several in one text count (OUTSIDE_SURFACE 0 0 INSIDE_SURFACE 70 0);
  • the outline: the faces, the centre and the bed;
  • the hardware groove seen from above, as wide as the group's hardware interface;
  • a similar profile's point, at the same distance from the nearer face (a proposal).

A point can also be typed (double-click Y or Z) or picked on the drawing (🎯, then click; it snaps to the nearest line end). ✔ confirms the selected points. Points confirmed or set by hand are never replaced by a new search.

Nothing unconfirmed is cut in production. A placement measured from a point nobody has confirmed stays proposed; one measured from confirmed points is worked out, fine for a trial piece. Only a placement a person confirmed goes into a production program.

Accessories. The upper table lists the accessories of the profile's group (➕ gives one to the group - those that fit its hardware interface come first; the tick switches one off without removing it). The lower table lists where each operation lands on this profile: the cut, the face the tool comes in by, Y and Z, the correction, where along the bar, and the depth with the walls the tool goes through. The selected one is drawn on the section: the arrow the tool comes in by, the cut in red and the walls in yellow.

Trial and tune. Move the selected placement with the Y and Z buttons (±0.1, 0.5, 1 mm) - the drawing shows where it would go. Keep the move for this profile, every profile of its group, or every profile of its series, for this operation or all its operations, and press 💾: the move is kept as a correction with its reason, and the accessory's own definition is not touched. A moved placement is to be confirmed again. ✖ marks an operation that is not on this profile, ↺ reopens a decision, 🕘 shows the history and 🧮 lists the corrections that reach the profile (and takes one away).

Programs.

  • Accessories: the side machining of one bar of the given length. ⚙ makes it with the confirmed placements - tick trial to include the unconfirmed ones for a test piece. Each operation takes the best mounted tool (a drill of the hole's size, else the largest end mill that fits), goes down in passes, enters each wall at its entry speed (slower still through a thin wall) and each chamber at the approach feed, and cuts at the wall's feed. What could not be made is listed in red under the program, with why. 💾 saves it as a file.
  • End milling: the contour milled around the profile's end, from the drawing. The drawing's tools choose and edit the border; the green band is the tool at its real width, and clicking it selects the matching line (and the other way round). 💾 keeps the program with the profile; 📤 saves it as a file.

The library's tables (📚): vendors, series, variants, groups, machining families and hardware interfaces; accessories with their operations (a macro, the face, the depth - the macro's, through the first wall or through the whole profile - the reference point, the offset and where along the bar), accessory groups, hardware vendors and series; macros, the shape of a cut only (no tool, no feed); and this machine's tools (their measured diameter and usable length - a tool with no diameter is never chosen), faces (which milling unit at which angle machines which face), feeds per material class, drawing layers that are another material (a steel reinforcement) and the program settings. Not confirmed yet lists every reference point and placement still to be checked; double-click one to open its profile.

The example catalogue offered when the library is new holds EXAMPLE positions, not a hardware maker's figures, and the machine faces are assumed (0° from above, 90° the outside face, 180° from below, 270° the inside face). Check both on the machine before the first program is run.

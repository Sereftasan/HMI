CYLINDER MOVEMENT FAILURE TROUBLESHOOTING MANUAL

CAUTION

When any alarm has occurred, eliminate its cause, ensure safety, and reset the alarm before restarting operation. Otherwise, it may cause injury.

Before any manual check on cylinders or moving parts, isolate and lock out all energy sources (electrical, pneumatic) and release stored pressure. A cylinder may move suddenly when air is restored. In 5/3 closed-center circuits and clamping circuits, pressure can remain trapped in the lines — exhaust BOTH sides of the cylinder before working.

Never bypass or force safety interlocks (guard doors, light curtains) to clear an alarm.

POINT

This document applies to every cylinder movement failure alarm regardless of direction. “Movement” below means the commanded stroke in either direction: forward/backward, up/down, clamp/release.

Follow the checks in order. Details 1.4 (valve) and 1.7 (feedback) are split by type: each type is shown with the figure and description on its left. These are NOT sequential steps — they are alternatives. Identify the valve and feedback type of the cylinder from the physical machine or from its electrical and pneumatic drawings, then apply only the rows of the matching type. Finish with detail 1.8.

If you are able to use the PLC online monitor, first see which output was commanded and which feedback signal was expected but missing, before checking hardware. If not, skip this and continue with the physical checks.

Some checks and actions require working in the PLC program, changing parameters, or replacing components. Perform them only if you are trained and authorized to do so — otherwise, stop and request support from maintenance / automation personnel.

After replacing a sensor, pressure switch, valve, or encoder, always re-test the movement at low speed / setup mode first. On encoder-equipped cylinders, perform the reference (homing) cycle after any repair and verify the position accuracy.

If the problem cannot be solved despite the checks above, please contact your distributor or Kaban Machine Technical Service.

Remedies for cylinder movement failure

Details 1.1–1.6 apply to every cylinder, in the order given. For details 1.4 (valve) and 1.7 (feedback), the “Detail name” column is split by type; each type is shown with the figure on its left. The types are alternatives — identify the valve and feedback type of the cylinder from the physical machine or from its electrical/pneumatic drawings, and apply only the matching rows. Finish with detail 1.8 (monitoring time).

Alarm:

Cylinder movement failure (any direction: forward/backward, up/down, clamp/release)

Alarm content

The movement command was issued, but the expected feedback — depending on the configuration: end sensor, pressure switch, analogue position signal, or encoder position window — was not confirmed within the monitoring time.

1.1

Load stuck / mechanical obstruction

(1) A workpiece, profile, chip, or foreign object is jammed in the cylinder path or in the tooling.

Lock out energy (LOTO) and exhaust BOTH sides of the cylinder. Visually inspect the full stroke path, tooling, and clamp jaws.

An obstruction is found.

Remove the obstruction. Check the tooling and guides for damage before restart.

No obstruction is found.

Go to check (2).

(2) The mechanism is binding (misalignment, lack of lubrication, bent rod, damaged guide/bearing).

With pressure exhausted, move the mechanism by hand through the full stroke.

Movement is stiff, notchy, or impossible.

Realign the guide, lubricate, or replace the damaged bearing/guide. Check the rod for bending and scoring.

Movement is smooth.

Go to check (3).

(3) The load exceeds the cylinder thrust (process load increased, regulator set too low).

Compare the actual load with the cylinder thrust at the set pressure (thrust = piston area × pressure − friction).

Thrust margin is insufficient.

Increase the regulator pressure within the rated limit, reduce the load, or select a larger bore cylinder.

Thrust margin is sufficient.

Check detail 1.2.

1.2

Supply pressure failure

(1) Air supply pressure is low or absent (compressor off, shut-off valve closed, filter clogged).

Read the pressure gauge at the FRL unit while the machine is commanding the movement.

Pressure is below the specified value (e.g. < 5 bar).

Restore the supply: open the shut-off valve, check the compressor, replace the clogged filter element, repair leaks.

Pressure is normal.

Go to check (2).

(2) A hose or fitting on the cylinder is leaking, kinked, or disconnected; a flow-control (speed) valve is fully closed; on clamping circuits, the dedicated clamp-line regulator is set too low.

Listen/inspect for leaks at both cylinder ports and along the tubing while the valve is actuated. Check the flow-control valves and the clamp-line regulator setting.

A leak, kink, or closed flow control is found.

Replace the damaged hose/fitting, remove the kink, re-set the flow control valve and the regulator.

No problem is found.

Check detail 1.3.

1.3

Solenoid valve does not switch (all valve types)

(1) The solenoid coil is not energized (wiring break, connector loose, coil burnt).

Command the movement and check the valve coil LED. Measure the voltage at the coil connector (e.g. 24 V DC). Press the manual override button on the valve.

LED off / no voltage at the coil, but the manual override moves the cylinder.

Repair the wiring/connector between the relay (or PLC output) and the coil. If voltage is present but the LED is off, replace the coil. Then check details 1.5/1.6.

LED on / voltage present, but the cylinder does not move.

Go to check (2).

(2) The valve spool is stuck or contaminated (dirty air, dried grease, worn seals).

Actuate the manual override several times and listen for the spool shifting. Check the exhaust ports for continuous blow-by.

The spool does not shift or there is heavy blow-by.

Clean or replace the solenoid valve. Check the air quality (filter, dryer, lubricator setting).

The valve works normally.

Check detail 1.4 (valve-type-specific).

1.5

Relay does not activate

(1) The interface relay coil is not energized (PLC output off, wiring break, blown fuse in the relay circuit).

Command the movement; check the relay LED / listen for the click. Measure the coil voltage.

No voltage at the relay coil.

Check the fuse and the wiring from the PLC output to the relay. Then check detail 1.6.

Coil voltage is present but the relay does not pull in.

Replace the relay (open coil).

(2) The relay pulls in but the contact does not conduct (burnt, oxidized, or worn contact).

With the relay energized, measure the voltage across the contact terminals (≈ 0 V when closed) and the voltage arriving at the valve coil.

Voltage is dropped across the contact / no voltage reaches the valve.

Replace the relay. If failures repeat, add a surge suppressor / flyback diode across the valve coil.

The contact conducts normally.

Check detail 1.6.

1.6

PLC output does not switch

(1) The output is not being commanded (interlock, safety condition, or sequence step not satisfied — e.g. guard door open, upstream sensor not made).

Monitor the output bit and its rung in the PLC (online monitor). Identify which contact in the rung is blocking the output.

The output bit stays OFF and a blocking condition is found.

Resolve the interlock condition (close the guard, satisfy the upstream sensor). NEVER bypass safety interlocks.

The output bit turns ON in the program.

Go to check (2).

(2) The physical output does not follow the logic (output channel failed, output card fuse blown, missing field power/common).

With the output bit ON, check the output card channel LED and measure the voltage at the output terminal. Check the card fuse and the field power supply to the common terminal.

No voltage at the terminal / fuse blown / LED off.

Replace the fuse or the output

channel/card. If the fuse blows

repeatedly, find the short circuit or overload (e.g. shorted coil) in the field wiring.

The terminal voltage is normal.

Check the wiring from the terminal to the relay/valve (details 1.3/1.5).

1.7

Two end magnetic sensors

A magnetic sensor at each end. Complete = target end ON, opposite end OFF. (Also applies to the back-end sensor of the other

configurations.)

(1) The end magnetic (reed) sensor is misadjusted, loose, or failed, so the movement completes but is not detected.

Move the cylinder to the target end (manual override) and check the sensor LED and the PLC input bit.

The cylinder is at the end but the LED/input is OFF.

Slide the sensor along the cylinder body until the LED turns on reliably, tighten the band clamp. If it still does not switch, replace the sensor.

LED is ON but the PLC input is OFF.

Repair the sensor cable/connector or the input card channel. Check the input common wiring.

(2) The opposite-end sensor stays ON (welded reed contact, short circuit in the cable, or on a short-stroke cylinder the magnet is sensed by both sensors).

Move the cylinder to each end and observe both sensor LEDs and PLC input bits at each position.

One sensor stays ON at both positions.

Replace the stuck sensor, repair the shorted cable, or re-position the sensors.

The sensors switch correctly.

Check detail 1.8 (monitoring time).

1.7

Single magnetic sensor (short stroke)

ON = at the sensor-side end, OFF = at the other end. The far end is confirmed only by OFF + time.

(1) The single magnetic sensor at the sensor-side end is misadjusted, loose, or failed; the cylinder reaches the sensor end but is not detected.

Move the cylinder to the sensor end (manual override); check the sensor LED and the PLC input.

At the sensor end but the LED/input is OFF.

Slide the sensor until the LED switches reliably, tighten the clamp; replace it if it still does not switch.

LED ON but the PLC input is OFF.

Repair the sensor cable/connector or the input channel; check the input common wiring.

(2) The sensor stays ON

continuously (welded reed contact, shorted cable); the other-end position (sensor OFF) is never seen.

Move the cylinder to the far end; verify the sensor turns OFF.

The sensor stays ON at the far end too.

Replace the stuck sensor or repair the shorted cable.

The sensor turns OFF at the far end.

Go to check (3).

(3) There is no sensor at the far end, so movement in that direction is confirmed only by sensor OFF + time; the sensor also reads OFF if the cylinder stops mid-stroke.

Visually verify that the movement to the far end actually completes; perform the mechanical/supply checks (1.1/1.2).

The cylinder does not reach the far end (stops mid-stroke).

Apply details 1.1/1.2 (mechanical obstruction / supply pressure).

The cylinder reaches the far end.

1.7

Single inductive sensor (short stroke)

Same logic as the single magnetic sensor; detects a metal target (flag). Sensitive to the sensing gap.

(1) The sensing gap is wrong or the metal target/flag has shifted or bent (inductive sensors are gap-sensitive).

Measure the gap between the sensor face and the target; check that the target flag is not bent or missing.

Gap too large / target shifted.

Set the gap within the nominal sensing distance (Sn); fix or replace the target. Non-ferrous targets reduce the sensing distance — select the correct sensor.

Gap and target are correct.

Go to check (2).

(2) The sensor is failed or shifted, a cable/connector fault, or the 3-wire sensor is wired incorrectly.

Move the cylinder to the sensor end and check the LED and the PLC input; verify the wiring.

At the end but LED/input OFF, or the wiring is wrong.

Adjust/replace the sensor; correct the wiring.

The sensor works correctly.

Go to check (3).

(3) No sensor at the far end; that direction is confirmed only by sensor OFF + time (same as the single magnetic sensor).

Visually verify that the movement to the far end completes; perform checks 1.1/1.2.

The cylinder does not reach the far end.

Apply details 1.1/1.2.

The cylinder reaches the far end.

Check detail 1.8.

1.7

Magnetic sensor + pressure switch (clamping)

Back-end magnetic sensor + pressure switch on the clamp line. Clamped = switch ON and back-end sensor OFF.

(1) The pressure switch setpoint is wrong (set higher than the clamp-line working pressure can ever reach, or the setpoint has drifted).

With the cylinder clamped, measure the clamp-line pressure with a gauge and compare it with the switch setpoint.

The setpoint is above the line pressure / has drifted.

Re-set the setpoint (typically slightly below the regulator setting). Replace a drifted/failed switch.

The setpoint is correct.

Go to check (2).

(2) Pressure builds and the switch turns ON, but the back-end magnetic sensor is still ON (the cylinder never left the released end: an obstruction at the start of the stroke, or the sensor is stuck).

Visually verify the actual rod position. Check the back-end sensor LED state.

The rod has not moved.

Remove the obstruction at the start of the stroke (detail 1.1). If pressure rises instantly without the cylinder resting on anything, look for mechanical seizure.

The rod has advanced but the sensor stays ON.

Replace the stuck back-end sensor (see the end magnetic sensor rows).

(3) The cylinder has mechanically clamped, but the pressure switch stays OFF: a leak on the clamp line (pressure cannot build), the switch has failed, or the switch is plumbed on the wrong line.

In the clamped state, measure the clamp-line pressure with a gauge; verify from the pneumatic diagram which line the switch is mounted on.

Pressure is low / a leak is found.

Find and repair the leak (hose, fitting, cylinder seal).

Pressure is normal but the switch is OFF.

Replace the pressure switch, or move it to the correct line.

(4) The switch signal chatters during holding (setpoint is marginal, supply pressure fluctuates).

Record the input bit with a high-speed trace; observe the supply pressure fluctuation.

The signal chatters.

Set the setpoint with a hysteresis margin, add a short ON-delay

(debounce) in the PLC, stabilize the supply.

The signal is stable.

Check detail 1.8.

1.7

Analogue magnetic sensor (4–20 mA)

Continuous position over the full stroke: one end 4 mA, the other end 20 mA.

(1) The analogue signal is out of range: a broken cable / failed sensor drops below 4 mA (or to 0); a short circuit rises above 20 mA.

Read the raw mA / scaled position value in the PLC during the movement.

mA < ~3.6 mA or 0.

Open circuit / broken cable / failed sensor: repair the cable or replace the sensor.

mA > ~21 mA.

Short circuit: check and repair the wiring.

mA within the 4–20 mA range.

Go to check (2).

(2) The signal changes but the scaling/calibration is wrong (the 4 mA and 20 mA ends were not taught, or the mechanics changed); the target window is never reached.

Compare the mA at both physical ends with the expected 4/20 mA; check the scaling parameters.

The end values have shifted.

Re-calibrate / re-teach the span; check the position window.

The scaling is correct.

Go to check (3).

(3) Noise on the analogue line (routed with power cables, shield not grounded) makes the position jitter; or the direction/polarity is reversed (4 mA at the wrong end).

Check the cable routing and the shield grounding; verify the mA at a known end.

The position jitters / the direction is reversed.

Separate the cable from power lines, ground the shield, add filtering; correct the scaling/wiring direction.

The signal is stable and correct.

Check detail 1.8.

1.7

Incremental encoder + reference sensor

Encoder + homing reference sensor at the back end. Position valid only after homing.

(1) The encoder count does not change although the cylinder moves (cable/connector break, encoder failure, mechanical fixing/coupling slipped).

Move the cylinder with the manual override and watch the live position value in the PLC. Check the cable, connectors, and the mechanical fixing of the encoder.

The count does not change.

Repair the cable, re-fix the

mounting/coupling, or replace the encoder. Then repeat the homing cycle.

The count changes.

Go to check (2).

(2) The count changes in the wrong direction or the position drifts (A/B channels swapped, slip, electrical noise — missing shield grounding).

Move in a known direction and verify the counting direction. Check the cable routing

(separation from power cables) and the shield grounding.

Direction is inverted / position drifts.

Correct the A/B wiring or the direction parameter; ground the shield at the specified end, separate the cable from power lines; tighten the mechanical connection.

Counting is correct.

Go to check (3).

(3) The reference (homing) sensor at the back end is failed or misadjusted: homing never completes after power-up, so the position is invalid and every movement alarms.

Check the “homing done” status bit in the PLC. Move the cylinder to the back end and watch the reference sensor LED.

Homing does not complete / the sensor does not switch.

Re-adjust or replace the reference sensor (see the end magnetic sensor rows); repeat the homing cycle.

Homing is completed.

Go to check (4).

(4) The target position window is too tight, or the target values are wrong after a mechanical change.

Compare the actually reached position with the target and window values in the PLC.

The position is near the target but outside the window.

Re-teach the target positions or widen the in-position window to a reasonable value.

The position is far from the target.

Re-check details 1.1–1.4

(mechanical/pneumatic); if the problem persists, contact your distributor or Kaban Machine Technical Service.

1.8

Monitoring time

(1) The monitoring time is too short for the actual movement time (flow controls drifted, load increased, speed reduced).

Measure the actual travel time and compare it with the alarm monitoring time in the PLC/HMI.

Travel time is close to or above the limit.

Re-adjust the flow-control valves to the specified speed, or increase the monitoring time to a suitable value after confirming it is safe.

Travel time is well within the limit.

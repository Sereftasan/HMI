2. Signing in and levels

Click the user icon on the caption bar, choose your name from the list and type your password. The name of the last person who signed in is already chosen and the cursor waits in the password box; Change user opens the list straight away. The icon stops pulsing once someone is signed in. Five wrong passwords lock that account for a minute.

LevelTypicallyCan
NoneNobody signed inWatch every page. Change nothing.
OperatorMachine operatorManual Movement, units of measure, acknowledge and reset alarms, most machine controls.
MaintenanceMaintenance / setterEverything an operator can, plus Machine Parameters (axis settings, alarm settings) and the logs.

Each controller also has its own level. A page may be open to you while a sub-machine's controls on it still need a higher one – the control's tooltip says so.

3. Moving between pages

  • Page menu (left): Home, Manual Movement, Alarms, Machine Parameters, HMI Settings – plus the machine's own pages (see 13).
  • Machine list (under it): when sub-machines are mounted, each page exists once per machine. Pick the machine here. The highlighted machine is always the one whose page is on screen.
  • Tabs: some pages have their own tabs along the top (HMI Settings, several Manual Movement pages).
  • ◀ Back / ▶ Forward: return to where you were – the same page, the same machine and the same tab. Choosing the page you are already on does not add a step.
  • Collapse the menu with the button at its top to icons only; drag its right edge to set its width. Both are remembered.

3a. The side bar and its pages

The bar at the right edge, from under the caption to the bottom, opens side pages – for what you watch or do alongside a page: the machine's inputs and outputs while you move a cylinder, the PLC Monitor while you test a unit, a machine's own monitoring or order pages. A side page opens beside the page on display, which narrows to make room (and scrolls when it no longer fits): nothing is ever covered.

  • Open / close: its icon on the bar (the open one is marked), or the ✕ on the page's header. Closing only hides it: it opens again exactly as you left it.
  • Share the screen: drag the border between the page and the side page. The width is remembered.
  • A window of its own (for a second screen): the button beside the ✕. The button on that window's header puts it back. It opens where it was last – in the panel or in its window.
  • Fold the bar to a thin edge with its top button, and back.
  • The bar shows only the pages your level may open, and is not there when there are none.

4. Manual Movement: the units

Every cylinder, motor and axis you can move by hand is a unit: a frame with its name on the top line, one or two buttons, and sometimes a position reading between them. Axis units and cylinder units are the same size and shape so a page reads as one table, and every position reads the same way: black bold figures on grey, as on the Axis Settings rows.

The buttons

  • Momentary (jog): the movement runs only while you hold the button. It stops when you let go – and also if the pointer is taken away by a message, the page changes, the button is hidden or disabled, or you are signed out.
  • Alternate: one press on, the next off (motors, cooling, blowing).
  • A button turns darker while its relay is on, and darker still with white text while the physical output is on.
  • The small dots on a button's outer corner are its sensors; rest the pointer on one to see which.
  • A slot with no function stays as an empty grey cell, so the buttons of neighbouring units line up.
Safety. A jog is released by the HMI in every case it can detect. The machine's own interlocks, emergency stop and the PLC program remain the protection – never rely on the screen to stop a movement.

The unit's ?

The ? does not blink – the part that failed does. One document opens straight away; several are offered as a list. A unit with nothing to open shows no mark until something fails. While an error bit is set, the part that failed blinks a red border: the button of the movement that failed (cylinder), or the position reading (axis). A controller that is not answering shows no ?: its reading cannot be trusted.

5. Entering a value

  • Touch a value field: the keypad opens beside it and the field is outlined in blue so you can see which one you are changing.
  • The line at the top gives the allowed range. A value outside it is refused (the entry flashes red) – nothing is sent.
  • Angles (the angle axes' settings) are always 0 – 360°. A minimum above the maximum is a range through zero: minimum 270, maximum 45 allows 270…360 and 0…45, and the line reads 270.000 ≤ … or … ≤ 45.000. A minimum below the maximum is an ordinary range. The field blinks when its value is outside, as any limited field does.
  • The first key replaces the whole value; ◀ ▶ move into it to change one digit.
  • Use the keypad's . key for decimals – on any Windows language setting. On a keyboard both . and , type the decimal point.
  • Values are typed in the unit shown on screen (mm or inch, °C or °F); the HMI converts them for the PLC.
  • Some fields ask for confirmation before writing.

6. Alarms

  • While a machine reports an error, the Alarms page button pulses red and the red alarm button appears on the caption bar.
  • The caption-bar alarm button lists the machines in alarm and how many alarms each has. Reset (n) Active Alarms resets that machine: it sends the machine's alarm reset on its own controller, like the Reset button on the Alarms page, and clears its list. An alarm whose cause is still there comes straight back. Operator If the reset cannot be sent – nobody signed in, controller not answering – the list is still cleared and a message says the machine itself was not reset.
  • The Alarms page shows the alarms of the machine selected in the machine list. The Help column opens the documents linked to an alarm; the Reset button drives the machine's reset device as the machine's program says: a short pulse, or – on the Processing Center (FA2010, M102) – SET: the HMI switches the device on and the PLC switches it off itself once every device is reset. An Admin sees the device and how it is driven in the button's tooltip.
  • Each machine has its own reset device, set in the machine's program. Where none is set yet, the Reset button is grey and says so, and the caption-bar menu clears the list without resetting the machine – nothing is pulsed on a guess.
  • Each alarm is watched on its own controller: a sub-machine's alarm comes from the sub-machine's PLC, never from the main machine's.
  • An alarm is only raised or cleared from a fresh reading. When a controller stops answering, its alarms are left as they were rather than cleared.

7. PLC connection

A controller counts as connected only while reads are succeeding. When one stops answering for three seconds:

  • the red link mark appears on the caption bar;
  • the controls of that controller grey out and say why, and its values are washed out – they are the last known values, not live ones;
  • it reconnects by itself when the controller answers again.

8. Units of measure

HMI Settings › Unit Settings Operator chooses the units for the whole program (length, speed, temperature...). Values are always stored in the machine's units and only shown in yours, so switching never changes a machine setting.

The magnifying factor

Draws the whole program larger or smaller – text, buttons, fields, menus, pages – from 0.75 (more on the screen) to 2 (twice the size). On User Settings:

  • For everyone: the panel's factor, for every user with none of their own. The program starts with it after the next restart (it offers to restart at once).
  • A user's own factor (empty = the panel's): when that user signs in and the screen is at another factor, the program asks whether to restart with theirs. The machine keeps running meanwhile, the screen is back in a few seconds, and the user stays signed in. The program then keeps starting with that factor until another is chosen, so the same operator after a power cut is not asked again.

Pages are designed for the panel's screen at factor 1: at a larger factor they no longer fit and scroll; at 1.5 or 2 on a small screen that means scrolling to reach a page's far side. Pictures (icons, drawings) are drawn larger but not sharper. Windows' own message boxes keep Windows' size.

10. Machine Parameters

Maintenance One tab per machine.

  • Axis Settings: one row per axis – jog, position, limits, reference, calibration, drive codes. The axis units on the Manual Movement pages take everything from these rows.
  • Drive codes (the last three columns): axis alarm and axis warning as the motion module's manual prints them (1904H), servo alarm as the amplifier's display shows it (16.3 = alarm 16, detail 3). A code that is not zero blinks and is a link (the pointer turns into a hand): a click opens just that code's entry of its manual – its causes, how to check each, and what to do. Warnings are amber, alarms red; a blank cell means no code.
  • Alarm Settings: the severity of each alarm and its helper link (a document, drawing or page opened from the alarm). Saving writes the machine's own Alarms.json, so each machine keeps its own settings.

11. Logs and diagnostics

  • Every change made from the HMI is written to the audit log with the time and the user: one file per day, HmiAudit_yyyy-MM-dd.txt.
  • Lines starting [PLC DIAG] record communication changes (a station lost, a station back).
  • Log Settings Maintenance shows the log as a table, newest first (click Time to turn it round):
    • Period: last hour, today, yesterday, 7 or 30 days, everything kept – or any From/To (changing either picks From - to).
    • 🔍 Search: every word typed must appear, in any case – D210 admin finds admin's entries about D210. User and Kind (sign-in, connection, PLC writes, problems, settings, other) narrow it further; ✖ clears them.
    • Live: while the period runs up to now, new entries appear as they are written; the row you are reading stays put.
    • The selected entry is shown in full under the table (a station scan is a report of several lines). 💾 exports the entries shown, as a log file or a CSV table.
    • Admin only: switching the log off (it asks first, and writes who switched it off; while it is off the page says so in red) and how many days are kept. 📂 opens the log folder.
  • PLC Diagnostics Maintenance:
    • Controllers: each controller with its driver and connection as running now, whether it answers, when it last did, the last error in words, and drops today – a link that drops many times a day points at a cable, a switch or a network, not the program.
    • Connection events: the log's connection entries, with the same filters.
    • MX Component Admin: the MX Component environment (where the driver is registered, which logical stations are stored); ▶ tests one logical station and 🔍 scans a range, in the background (■ stops). A logical station the machine is connected through is not opened a second time – its live state is shown instead.
  • PLC Monitor Admin: a side page (3a) – its icon on the side bar, F12 (again to close) or the user menu. Reads and writes any device of any controller – it bypasses every control's own checks, so use it with care.
    • Every button says what it does when the pointer rests on it.
    • Make it as narrow as you like (drag the side panel's border, or size its own window): the buttons that no longer fit go under the chevron (») at the end of the toolbar.
    • One tab per controller; under each, your own tabs (📑): + after the last tab adds one at the end; the tab menu (right-click a tab) adds one before or after the one on display; rename (or double-click the tab); delete – every tab can go, and adding a device then makes a Main tab for it. Drag a tab along the strip to move it, as many places as you like.
    • 🗃 adds every device used by: the screen on display; the whole program – with a heading line per page, or as one list (each device once, so a click on Address sorts every device the HMI uses); the whole program as one tab per page; or every device named in the PLC label list – PlcLabels.csv beside the program: the PLC programmer's names for its devices, which name the devices on the monitor and include devices no page shows.
    • 🔎 Find (Ctrl+F, or right-click a row: Where is it used?): type an address, a label or part of a unit's name. Every use is listed – twice if a page uses it twice – with its page, in a pane under the lists (✕ or Esc closes it). Click a line: the page opens beside the monitor and the unit is framed in blinking blue. Drag its headings into another order and its borders to size them: kept for next time.
    • ➕ adds a device at the end or above the selected row – or an array: M100, 10 of them adds M100…M109; a double word counts in twos (D200, D202…). A comment line has only text: a heading for the rows under it.
    • Copy (two pages) copies the selected rows (Ctrl+C), Paste (the clipboard) pastes them above the selected row (Ctrl+V) or at the end; the arrows to a bar (⤒ ⤓) or dragging re-order (Ctrl+↑/↓). A right-click on a selection of several rows keeps them all selected, so Copy and Remove take them all.
    • 🗑 deletes: the selected rows (Del), this tab, every tab of this controller, or every tab of every controller (these two ask first).
    • ↶ Undo (Ctrl+Z) takes back the last change to the lists – a sort, a delete, a paste, a move, a name or address typed, a tab added, renamed, moved or deleted – one step at a time. It is also how a list gets back its order from before a sort. A value written to the PLC is not undone. ↷ Redo (Ctrl+Y or Ctrl+Shift+Z) puts back what Undo took away, until the next change. Both buttons name the step in their tooltip.
    • 📤 exports the tab to a CSV file, 📥 imports devices from one (a PlcLabels.csv works as it is).
    • Columns: drag a heading to put the column elsewhere, drag its border to size it – the others keep their widths, and every tab uses the same layout. Click a heading to sort on it, again to reverse; under comment lines each section is sorted on its own and keeps its heading. Addresses sort in the PLC's order (M2 before M10).
    • Double-click a value (or Enter) to change it right there in the table; on anything else it opens that row with the field selected. F2 edits the cell in place.
    • Keys on the tab strip: Ins new tab, F2 rename, Del delete, Ctrl+←/→ move, Ctrl+Tab next tab. Tab moves between the table and the tab strips.
    • On a simulated controller every device the simulator holds is listed by itself; one you remove stays off the list until 👁 Show removed devices again.

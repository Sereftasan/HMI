## 1. Please review first: changes that affect how the machine behaves

| # | Change | Why | Where |

## 4t. The Profiles page on the profile library (30 September)

What your document asks the page to be, and what it now is. **Nothing on it is sent to the PLC**: it keeps data and makes program files. Changing anything needs the Maintenance level.

1. **One library for profiles and accessories** (`Kaban.Profiles`, a project of its own, SQLite file `ProgramData\KabanHMI\Kaban.db`, on the operating system's own SQLite - `winsqlite3.dll` on Windows 10 and later, no package to install). Filed **vendor > series > variant > group > profile**; a profile can be **based on** another (it takes its group, sizes and reference points; only what differs is entered). On first opening it asks whether to load the **example catalogue** (several hardware makers with EXAMPLE positions, and the page's three DECEUNINCK profiles). The old page's PostgreSQL table and its welding fields (melting, welding, temperature, clamping) are gone: they were never FA2010's.
2. **The drawing** (DXF) read in profile coordinates: Y across (0 at the outside face, on the left), Z up (0 at the bed). A drawing made another way is **turned or mirrored** (kept with the profile); the page compares the drawing's size with the profile's and says **"it lies on its side - Turn it"** when it does - the sample `82_Disa_Kapi.dxf` does (115 x 82 for an 82 x 115 profile). Layers that are not the profile (dimensions, a gasket) are **left out** of it: drawn grey, never cut through. The DXF reader is new (blocks and inserts, polylines with bulges - the old one drew their arcs on the wrong side - splines, ellipses, texts and points).
3. **Reference points** (outside and inside face, centre, bed, hardware axis, glazing reference, rebate line, chamber centre) **found on the drawing**, in order of trust: a layer named after the point (`REF_HARDWARE_AXIS`), a text naming it (`HARDWARE_AXIS 22 35` - several in one text, as in your example), the outline, the **hardware groove** seen from above (as wide as the group's interface, within 15 %), and - as a *proposal* - a **similar profile's** point at the same distance from the nearer face (similarity from a fingerprint of the section). They can be typed, or **picked on the drawing** (it snaps to a line end), and are **confirmed** by a person. A new search never replaces a point confirmed or set by hand. This is the "AI recognition as suggestion + confirmation": deterministic geometry, every proposal saying how it was found.
4. **Accessories** given to a **group** (every profile of it takes them; those fitting the group's hardware interface are offered first; one can be switched off without removing it). Each operation lands on the profile at **reference + offset + series, group and profile corrections**, from a **face** (top, bottom, outside, inside) and to a **depth** (the macro's, through the first wall, or through the whole profile). The table shows each placement with its depth and **the walls the tool goes through**; the drawing shows the arrow it comes in by, and for the selected one the cut (red) and the walls (yellow).
5. **Trial and tune**: move a placement by 0.1 / 0.5 / 1 mm in Y and Z (the drawing shows where it would go), and **keep the move for this profile, its group or its series**, for the operation or the whole accessory, with a reason - a **correction**; the accessory's definition is never changed. A moved placement is to be confirmed again; confirmations, rejections ("not on this profile") and moves are kept in each placement's **history**; a correction can be taken away (its placements go back and are to be confirmed again, on every profile it reached).
6. **Statuses, as the document says**: *proposed* (measured from an unconfirmed point), *worked out* (from confirmed points - fine for a trial piece), *confirmed* (a person), *not on this profile*. **Only confirmed placements go into a production program**; a **trial** program takes every one not rejected and says so in its header.
7. **The side machining program** (Programs > Accessories): every operation at its places along a bar of the given length (from the start, the end, centred, from both ends, spread by pitch), **the best mounted tool** (a drill of the hole's size, else the largest end mill that fits the slot or pocket, on a unit that machines that face, long enough), depth passes, and the feeds **following the drawing's levels**: rapid to just above the first wall, **each wall entered at its entry speed** (slower still when thin), **each chamber at the approach feed**, cut at the wall's feed; a wall on a layer mapped to another material (a steel reinforcement) takes that material's feeds. Incremental (G91, X/Y the plane, Z the penetration) or absolute. What it could not make is listed in red with why (no tool diameter set, no unit for that face, off the profile ...). Saved as `.nc`.
8. **The end milling program** (the contour around the profile's end - your existing G-code) is kept as it was, on the same drawing and tools, and is now **kept with the profile** (per machine) instead of a column of the old table.
9. **The library's tables** (📚): vendors, series, variants, groups, machining families, hardware interfaces; accessories with their operations, accessory groups, hardware vendors and series; **macros** with their shape entered by type (diameter, length, width, depth, corner radius ...); type defaults; **this machine**: tools (U1-T1, U1-T2, U2-T1, U2-T2 - **their diameters are empty until measured**, and a tool with no diameter is never chosen), faces, feeds, material classes, drawing layers, program settings. **Not confirmed yet** lists every point and placement still to check; double-click opens its profile.
